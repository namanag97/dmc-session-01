Step 1. Ask what the number is for.We asked the CEO one question before touching any data.

We askedThe CEO said
What will you use this number for?The monthly business review. How much did we sell in August that we get to keep?
So that means…Goods that reached customers. Not orders that were cancelled or came back. Not the tax we collect for the government.
What about orders still on the way?Tell me how many. Don't count them yet.

Step 7. State the conclusion and its limits.

August net revenue · excluding GST Goods delivered in August, less returns received in August. Supports: "we delivered and kept ₹2.2 Cr of sales in August." Down from the ₹3.3 Cr on the store dashboard by cancellations, undelivered COD, GST, and timing.
What this number does not prove

Not cash: of COD is still with couriers.

Not final: orders in transit; marketplace returns arrive for another month.

Not profit: fees, shipping, returns cost and ads are not in it.

Not demand: cancellations and RTO are excluded, so it understates what customers wanted.

Two valid views of August. Both right, different questions.

August activity

Everything that happened in August, whenever the order was placed.

JULAUGSEP

Counts the part of each order that falls inside the window. Our revenue number is this view.

August cohort

Orders placed in August, followed wherever they go, even into September.

JULAUGSEP

Counts whole orders that started in the window. Return rates and repeat rates need this view.

The exercise. Each team receives three things.Nothing else. No documents, no definitions, no hints. Everything you need is in the data.

01One message from the CEOOne line. Names a metric, not a definition. Same style as "What was our revenue in August?"
02The company's dataOne download. Same file for every team. A README explains every table in plain language.
03An AI analystChatGPT. Upload the file, ask questions in plain English. It computes; you decide what counts.
